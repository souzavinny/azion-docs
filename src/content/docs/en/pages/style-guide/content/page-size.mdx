## Write for retrieval as well as for readers

People are not the only readers of the documentation. Agents fetch each page as a markdown twin, served at the page URL plus `.md`. A retrieval system will split every page into chunks and embed each chunk separately.

That changes how you write, not just how much. A page can read well from top to bottom and still fail when a system serves one fragment of it.

## Keep sections and pages inside the limits

The limits apply to the page body and exclude the frontmatter.

| Unit | Target | Hard cap |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A `##` section | 2,000 characters | 4,000 characters |
| A whole page | 8,000 characters | 16,000 characters |

English prose runs at roughly 4 characters per token, and Portuguese runs closer to 3.5. So a 2,000-character section is about one retrieval chunk, and an 8,000-character page is three or four.

The targets describe what good pages here already do. The median section in the corpus is 751 characters, and the median page is 4,778. The targets sit near the 75th percentile. Over the hard cap, split the page.

## Understand what retrieval returns

A retrieval system does not return a page. It returns a chunk, usually one section, with no neighbors attached.

A 129,000-character page becomes 30 to 60 chunks. Any one of them, read alone, is a fragment: pronouns with no referent, steps with no goal, a table with no caption. The retriever finds the fragment, an agent quotes it, and the answer is wrong in a way nobody traces back to the page.

An 8,000-character page becomes three or four chunks. Each chunk still describes one recognizable thing.

## Write every section to stand alone

Every `##` section must make sense to a reader who has not seen the rest of the page. Five habits make that true:

- Name the subject in each section. Write "Cache stores content at Azion's distributed network", not "It caches content there". A pronoun whose referent sits two sections up breaks the moment the section travels alone. Once the section has named its subject, a definite noun phrase or a pronoun pointing at the previous sentence is correct and preferred: this rule guards the chunk boundary, not every sentence inside it.
- Remove back-references. "As mentioned above" and "the previous step" assume context the chunk does not have. When a section depends on an earlier one, name that section.
- Write headings as search queries. "Configure cache TTL" retrieves; "Configuration" does not, and "Step 2" never will. Lead with the imperative verb, which is also what a reader types into search. See [Headings](/en/documentation/style-guide/formatting/headings/).
- Keep a table with the sentence that explains it. A table split from its caption is noise. Give a long table its own heading.
- Write one topic per paragraph, six sentences at most. A paragraph that covers two topics chunks badly wherever the splitter cuts it. The paragraph rules are in [Sentence structure](/en/documentation/style-guide/writing/sentence-structure/).

## Cut padding, keep the grammar

Verbose prose wastes the reader's time, and it also dilutes the chunk. An embedding of 2,000 characters where 600 carry the information matches worse than an embedding of 600 that are all signal. A preamble that restates the heading is the first thing to cut.

Concision has a hard floor. Never drop a subject, a verb, or an article to save characters. A clipped sentence trades length for ambiguity, which is the opposite of the point. Split the sentence instead.

## Apply the exemptions

Four kinds of content legitimately exceed the caps. Do not split them to satisfy a number:

- Complete reference tables. A full list of 200 status codes is one thing and belongs together. Long is correct; incomplete is not.
- `<Tabs>` blocks. The Azion Console, CLI, and API paths for one task form a single section by construction, and they retrieve better together. Expect these sections to land between 2,000 and 4,000 characters.
- Changelogs and release notes. They are append-only by nature.
- Legal agreements. They are single documents, and a split changes their meaning.

Everything else over the cap is over the cap because the page does too much. Split the page, and choose a [content type](/en/documentation/style-guide/content/choose-a-content-type/) for each new page.
