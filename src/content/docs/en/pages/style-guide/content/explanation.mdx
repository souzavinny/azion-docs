## Purpose

An explanation page builds understanding of a product or a design. The reader is not in the middle of a task; they want to understand the design before they decide to use it.

## When to use

Write an explanation page when:

- The reader wants the reason the product works this way, not the steps.
- A how-to guide grows a long preamble, or a reference page stops to justify a design.
- The page must compare alternatives and name a tradeoff.

Do not write an explanation page when:

- The reader needs to look up a value, a field, or a limit. Write a [reference page](/en/documentation/style-guide/content/reference/).
- The reader needs steps for a task. Write a [how-to guide](/en/documentation/style-guide/content/how-to-guides/) or a [tutorial](/en/documentation/style-guide/content/tutorials/).

Explanation is the content type most often missing from the documentation. When another content type starts to explain, write the explanation page and keep that type clean.

## Structure

**Required components**

- **Lead paragraph**: the problem the design solves, plus one sentence that names the intended audience.
- **The approach**: how the design solves the problem.
- **The tradeoff**: what the design costs, and when it is the wrong choice.
- **What to read next**: links to the pages that implement or extend the design.

**Optional components**

- **Architecture diagram**: the diagram, then a paragraph that reads it.
- **Dataflow**: a numbered walkthrough of what moves where.
- **Components**: what each part does and why it is there.
- **Implementation**: links to the how-to guides; this section links, it does not instruct.

An architecture page uses all the optional components, in the order above. An explanation that does not describe an architecture takes a looser shape; the required components still apply.

## Template

The template follows the architecture shape. Copy it and replace each `<placeholder>`.

```md
<The problem this design solves, and what it works against.> This document is for <the intended audience> who want to <what that audience wants to do>.

## <Design name> architecture diagram

![<What the diagram shows>](/assets/docs/images/uploads/<diagram-file>.png)

<A paragraph that reads the diagram: what to look at first, and what depends on what.>

### <Design name> dataflow

1. <What moves first, and where it goes.>
2. <What the platform does with it.>
3. <Where the flow ends.>

## Components

- **<Component name>**: <what it does and why it is there>.
- **<Component name>**: <what it does and why it is there>.

## Implementation

- [<How-to guide that implements this design>](<guide URL>)

## Related docs

- [<Related page>](<page URL>)
```

An explanation that does not describe an architecture drops the diagram and the dataflow. It keeps the problem, the approach, the tradeoff, and the links.

## Explain the why, not the how

Steps live in [tutorials](/en/documentation/style-guide/content/tutorials/) and [how-to guides](/en/documentation/style-guide/content/how-to-guides/), and the reader can find them there. What the reader cannot find anywhere else is the reason the product works this way. State the problem the design solves, what the design works against, and when it is the wrong choice.

Do not recreate material that is freely available elsewhere. Explain Azion's perspective on the topic, and link out for the general background.

Do not include steps. The moment a numbered click-through appears, two content types share one page. Link to the guide instead.

## Name the alternatives and the tradeoff

An explanation page is the only place in the documentation where alternatives belong. "You could also use X, but that trades Y for Z" is the sentence this content type exists for. The same sentence inside a how-to guide means the guide has lost the plot.

Every architectural choice costs something, so name the cost. An explanation that presents only upsides is marketing, not documentation.

## Discuss, do not enumerate

An explanation discusses a design; it does not list its settings. A page that turns into a table of fields wants to be a [reference page](/en/documentation/style-guide/content/reference/). Move the table there and keep the reasoning.

Avoid claims that expire. "Currently", "at the time of writing", and "will soon" all age badly, and nobody comes back to fix them. Write what the product does, without a date attached.

## Write descriptive sentences

Explanation is descriptive text, so its sentences cap at 25 words. This type breaches the cap most often, because reasoning about tradeoffs invites subordinate clauses. Split a long sentence instead of compressing it.

Passive voice is allowed here, narrowly. Use it only when the actor is unknown or is the platform itself. "A cache policy is applied to the request" hides who applies the policy. "Rules Engine applies a cache policy to the request" does not. The full sentence rules are in [Sentence structure](/en/documentation/style-guide/writing/sentence-structure/).

## Let diagrams carry the explanation

Diagrams carry most of the load on an explanation page. A design you cannot draw is a design you do not understand well enough to explain yet.

Reference each image with a root-absolute path and no language prefix, such as `/assets/docs/images/uploads/content-delivery-dataflow.png`. Write alt text that describes what the diagram shows. "Dataflow for content delivery at the edge" prepares the reader; "architecture diagram" does not.
