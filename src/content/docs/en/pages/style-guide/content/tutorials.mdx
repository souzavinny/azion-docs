## Purpose

A tutorial teaches the product by having the reader build something that works. The author chooses the goal, guarantees the result, and keeps every decision away from the reader.

## When to use

- The reader is new and does not yet know what they want. You pick a goal for them, and they learn the product on the way to it.
- Do not use a tutorial for a reader who arrived with a task: that page is a [how-to guide](/en/documentation/style-guide/content/how-to-guides/).
- When in doubt, [Choose a content type](/en/documentation/style-guide/content/choose-a-content-type/) has the router.

## Structure

**Required components**

- **Opening paragraph**: what the reader will build and roughly how long it takes.
- **Before you start**: the minimum real prerequisites, each a link or a one-line command.
- **Numbered steps**: one instruction each, each ending with a visible result.
- **What you built**: one paragraph that restates the result.
- **Next steps**: two or three links, each with a reason to click it.

**Optional components**

- **One concept sentence** with a link to an [explanation](/en/documentation/style-guide/content/explanation/) page, when a concept is genuinely required.
- **An image** as the visible result of a step, when words alone cannot show it.

## Template

Copy the template and replace each `<placeholder>`.

```md
[One paragraph: what the reader will build,
and roughly how long it takes.]

## Before you start

- <A prerequisite the page cannot supply, as a link or a one-line command>

## Step 1: <verb phrase>

<What to do. Then what the reader should see.>

## Step 2: <verb phrase>

<...>

## What you built

<Restate the result. One paragraph.>

## Next steps

- <Link>: <the reason to click it>
- <Link>: <the reason to click it>
```

## Keep the promise: every step works

A tutorial makes one promise: if the reader follows every step in order, the result works. That promise creates four obligations:

- You choose the goal. Not "build an application", but "deploy a static site and see it live on your own URL".
- You choose every option. No "depending on your needs", no "you may prefer". The reader is here to learn, not to choose.
- Every step produces a visible result, so the reader knows they are on track.
- Every command runs. Test every command and every snippet before the page ships.

A tutorial that fails at step 7 is worse than no tutorial. It teaches the reader that the product is broken.

## Do not branch

A tutorial has one path. If a step can go two ways, pick one way and say nothing about the other. Alternatives belong in a [how-to guide](/en/documentation/style-guide/content/how-to-guides/), where the reader has a real situation to match them against.

Tabs are a branch, so tutorials do not use tabs. If the task genuinely needs three interfaces, the page is a how-to.

The phrases "you can also" and "depending on your setup" are the warning signs. When one of them appears in your draft, you left the tutorial and started a how-to.

## Do not explain

The tutorial reader does not retain explanations, and each interruption costs momentum. If a concept is genuinely required, give it one sentence and link to an [explanation](/en/documentation/style-guide/content/explanation/) page.

Keep asides rare for the same reason. A tutorial full of note blocks is a tutorial that tries to be reference material.

## Show the result of every step

End each step with what the reader sees: command output, a screenshot, a URL to open, or a value that changed. The reader must know they are on track without asking anyone.

A step with an invisible effect makes the reader lose confidence, because they cannot tell a correct state from a broken one.

## Write steps in the procedural register

Tutorials are procedural, so they take the tighter sentence budget: 20 words per sentence, one instruction per step, imperative voice. [Sentence structure](/en/documentation/style-guide/writing/sentence-structure/) holds the full rules.

The budget is tight because this reader pays the highest price for a misread step. They are new, they execute one step at a time, and they have no way to recover alone.

- Incorrect: `Open the file and change the TTL to 3600, then save and purge the cache.`
- Correct: four numbered steps, one instruction each.

## Keep tutorials scarce

Write one tutorial per product area. Tutorials are expensive to maintain, because every command must still run after every release. A product with five tutorials has five things to break on the next release.
